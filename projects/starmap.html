Starmap

A navigable 3D map of the universe in the browser, built entirely from real catalogues. Every object sits at its measured position and is drawn at its real physical size, from the 109-metre ISS to galaxy clusters 300 million light-years away. Click anything to read where the numbers came from.

How to use it

It opens looking at the solar system. Everything below works the same in the frame above and on the live site.

DragOrbit around whatever you are focused on.
ScrollZoom in and out. The scale bar at the bottom right tells you what you are looking at.
ClickSelect an object. The right-hand panel gives you its measurements, a photograph where one exists, and the catalogue it came from.
Double-clickFly there. The camera travels to the object and re-centres on it.
/Search 1.2 million objects by name. Try Betelgeuse, ISS, M87* or TRAPPIST-1e.
FWASDFree flight. Speed scales with how close you are to the nearest surface, so it works at both ends of the scale.
.Pause time. Satellites and orbits freeze where they are.
[]Change time speed, up to years per second. Asteroid positions are solved on the GPU, so it stays smooth.
HHelp, the full list of controls without leaving the app.

Your first five minutes

If you only do one thing, do step three.

  1. Let the catalogues load.

    The loading bar is reading real star, galaxy and satellite data. It is a lot of it, so the first run is the slow one.

  2. Double-click a planet.

    You will fly to it and stop at a sensible distance. The planets and large moons use real NASA and USGS surface maps.

  3. Press / and search for something you know.

    ISS puts you next to a design-accurate model propagated live with SGP4. Sgr A* takes you to the black hole at the centre of the galaxy, drawn at its measured Schwarzschild radius.

  4. Use Quick travel in the left panel.

    The big jumps are one click: the Milky Way, the Local Group, and "Observable universe", which pulls back to nearly a million quasars mapped to redshift z ≈ 7.

  5. Turn layers off.

    The left panel toggles every layer. Hiding satellites or the Gaia deep field cuts the clutter and lifts the frame rate on a weaker machine.

  6. Speed up time with ].

    Watch the satellite shells rotate, the planets run their orbits, and the comets swing through perihelion.

Honesty note. The info panel always tells you which numbers are measured and which are derived, and names the catalogue. Where an object has no photograph, anything illustrative is labelled as an impression rather than passed off as an observation.

How the scale works

World coordinates are heliocentric ICRF/J2000 in kilometres, kept in double precision on the CPU. The camera always sits at the origin and every object is re-expressed relative to it each frame, which is what stops the far-away things from juddering. A logarithmic depth buffer then covers one metre to 10²² kilometres in a single pass, so a satellite bolt and a quasar can be on screen at once.

Why that matters. Most space apps quietly switch scenes between "solar system" and "galaxy". Starmap does not. You can fly from the ISS to the edge of the observable universe without a loading screen or a change of units.

Run it yourself

# the app
git clone https://github.com/maxwitanowski/Starmap.git
cd Starmap
npm install
npm run dev        # http://127.0.0.1:5174

# rebuild the catalogues from raw/
npm run data
npm run dss        # deep-sky photographs

The published site is the built app on the gh-pages branch, produced with npm run build:pages, which strips the optional API keys out of the bundle.
